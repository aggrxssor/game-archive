FAQ – Frequently Asked Questions

I can’t launch a game. Why?

You must be registered and logged in to play any game on this platform. All games require authentication, regardless of type.

Do I need an account to use the site?

Yes. An account is required to play games. Browsing the site may be possible without logging in, but launching games is restricted to registered users only.

Why does a game download instead of launching?

Some games are downloadable titles, not browser-based. If a download starts, that means the game is meant to be installed and run locally.

What operating systems are supported?

Downloadable games currently target Windows. Browser-based games work on any modern browser on any operating system.

A downloaded game won’t start. What should I check?

Common causes include missing dependencies, antivirus interference, or incomplete extraction. Make sure you fully extract the game files before launching.

What dependencies do games require?

Depending on the game, you may need:

  • Visual C++ Redistributables
  • .NET Runtime
  • DirectX (mainly for older titles)

Required dependencies are listed on the game’s page when applicable.

Are the games safe?

All games are reviewed before being listed. Files are distributed without installers, bundled software, or hidden executables.

However, Windows may still show warnings for unsigned applications. This is normal for small or indie projects.

Why does Windows say “Unknown Publisher”?

Most indie and hobby projects are not code-signed. This warning does not automatically mean the game is unsafe.

Why don’t I see my score on the leaderboard?

Saving highscores, as of yet, is done by the user manually. We're working on automating this process.

Other possible reasons:

  • The game does not support score submission yet
  • Score submission is disabled for that title

How do I upload a highscore?

You can upload your highscores on the Leaderboards page, by copying and pasting the encoded highscore after a game has finished.

Can I submit my own game?

Yes. Game submissions and requests can be made through the request page. All submissions are reviewed manually before being added.

Is this an online multiplayer platform?

No. All games are single-player or local multiplayer only. There is no real-time online multiplayer functionality.

Why is the site missing features?

The platform is under active development. Features are added gradually, with a focus on stability, clarity, and long-term maintainability.

Will sound effects play automatically?

No. Any sound features require user interaction and can always be disabled. The site does not autoplay sound.

What is this platform for?

This site is a curated game archive and platform focused on preservation, experimental projects, and clean presentation — not ads, launchers, or background services.

About Us

This platform is a web-based game library and digital archive created to preserve, modernize, and present classic and experimental games in a clean, accessible environment.


The core idea is simple: after registering and logging in, users can access a curated collection of games and play them either alone against artificial intelligence or locally with others using split-screen gameplay.


The project goes beyond being a simple game launcher. It also functions as a digital archive, aiming to prevent classic games from disappearing due to outdated technology, unsupported platforms, or lack of long-term maintenance.


By reimplementing and modernizing these games using current technologies, the platform ensures they remain playable, understandable, and maintainable in the long term, without losing their original identity.


The game selection focuses primarily on logic, strategy, and arcade-inspired titles such as Chess, Battleship (Torpedo), and Space Shooter. Some games run directly in the browser, while others are provided as downloadable executables for local play.

This mixed approach allows each game to be delivered in the form that best suits its technical and gameplay requirements.


The platform is developed as part of an educational project, with a strong emphasis on clean frontend architecture, modular design, and long-term extensibility. The goal is not rapid feature expansion, but a stable and understandable system that can evolve over time.


Development is carried out by a small student development team as part of a formal project assignment, focusing on modern web technologies, software structure, and maintainable design practices.


The project was commissioned in a conceptual sense by a fictional organization specializing in digital game development and archiving, whose mission is to preserve classic games and make them accessible in modern environments for educational and cultural purposes.


Over time, the platform is intended to grow into a reliable archive where both classic and original games can coexist, serving players, learners, and anyone interested in the preservation of digital game heritage.
